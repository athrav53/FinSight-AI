INVESTMENT FUNDAMENTALS GUIDE

WHAT IS INVESTING?

Investing is the process of allocating money
to assets with the expectation of generating
income or increasing their value over time.

Investments involve risk, and returns are
not guaranteed.

SAVING VS INVESTING

Saving generally involves keeping money
in relatively accessible financial accounts.

Investing involves purchasing assets that
may increase or decrease in value.

Savings are often used for short-term goals
and emergency funds.

Investments may be appropriate for longer-term
financial goals depending on risk tolerance.

COMMON TYPES OF INVESTMENTS

1. Stocks:
   Represent ownership in a company.

2. Bonds:
   Represent lending money to an issuer
   in exchange for agreed payment terms.

3. Mutual Funds:
   Pool money from multiple investors
   to purchase a portfolio of assets.

4. Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs):
   Investment funds that trade on exchanges.

5. Fixed Deposits:
   Deposit products that generally offer
   predetermined interest terms.

6. Real Estate:
   Investment in land or property.

RISK AND RETURN

Investment risk refers to the possibility
of losing money or receiving returns
different from expectations.

Higher expected returns generally require
accepting greater investment risk.

However, taking greater risk does not
guarantee higher returns.

RISK TOLERANCE

Risk tolerance describes an individual's
willingness and ability to accept
investment losses or fluctuations.

Risk tolerance may depend on:

1. Financial goals
2. Investment time horizon
3. Income stability
4. Existing financial obligations
5. Financial knowledge
6. Ability to absorb losses

DIVERSIFICATION

Diversification means spreading investments
across different assets or asset classes.

Diversification can reduce exposure
to risks associated with individual assets.

However, diversification cannot eliminate
all investment risks.

COMPOUND INTEREST

Compound interest occurs when interest
is calculated on both the initial principal
and previously accumulated interest.

COMPOUND INTEREST FORMULA

Future Value = P * (1 + r)^n

Where:

P = Initial principal
r = Interest rate per compounding period
n = Number of compounding periods

EXAMPLE

Initial Investment: Rs. 10,000
Annual Interest Rate: 10%
Investment Period: 2 years

Future Value = 10,000 * (1 + 0.10)^2

Future Value = Rs. 12,100

Total Interest Earned = Rs. 2,100

This example assumes annual compounding,
a fixed interest rate, and no additional
contributions, fees, or taxes.

RETURN ON INVESTMENT

Return on Investment (ROI) measures
investment gain or loss relative
to the initial investment cost.

ROI Formula:

ROI = ((Final Value - Initial Cost)
       / Initial Cost) * 100

EXAMPLE

Initial Investment: Rs. 20,000
Final Investment Value: Rs. 24,000

ROI = ((24,000 - 20,000) / 20,000) * 100

ROI = 20%

This simplified calculation excludes
transaction fees, taxes, and other costs.

INVESTMENT TIME HORIZON

Investment time horizon refers to the
expected duration before invested money
will be needed.

Shorter time horizons may limit the ability
to recover from market fluctuations.

Longer time horizons may allow investors
to tolerate some short-term volatility,
but losses remain possible.

INFLATION AND INVESTMENTS

Inflation reduces the purchasing power
of money over time.

Investment returns should be considered
alongside inflation, taxes, and fees.

A positive nominal investment return
does not always represent an increase
in purchasing power.

IMPORTANT INVESTMENT RISKS

1. Market risk
2. Credit risk
3. Liquidity risk
4. Inflation risk
5. Interest rate risk
6. Currency risk

INVESTMENT BEST PRACTICES

1. Understand investment products before investing.
2. Define financial goals and time horizons.
3. Evaluate risk tolerance.
4. Consider diversification.
5. Review investment fees and charges.
6. Avoid unrealistic guaranteed-return promises.
7. Review investments periodically.
8. Maintain appropriate emergency savings.

ROLE OF AI IN INVESTMENT ANALYSIS

AI systems can analyze historical market data,
identify patterns, and summarize information.

However, historical patterns do not
guarantee future investment performance.

AI-generated predictions may be inaccurate.
