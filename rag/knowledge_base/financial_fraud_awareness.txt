FINANCIAL FRAUD AWARENESS GUIDE

WHAT IS FINANCIAL FRAUD?

Financial fraud involves dishonest or deceptive
activities intended to obtain money, financial
information, or other financial benefits.

Financial fraud can affect individuals,
businesses, and financial institutions.

COMMON TYPES OF FINANCIAL FRAUD

1. Phishing:
   Fraudulent emails or messages designed to
   trick people into revealing sensitive information.

2. Identity Theft:
   Unauthorized use of another person's
   personal information.

3. Card Fraud:
   Unauthorized use of debit or credit card details.

4. Investment Scams:
   Deceptive investment opportunities that may
   promise unrealistic or guaranteed returns.

5. Online Shopping Scams:
   Fraudulent sellers or websites that collect
   payments without delivering legitimate goods.

6. Account Takeover:
   Unauthorized access to a financial account.

POTENTIAL WARNING SIGNS

1. Transactions that the account holder
   does not recognize.

2. Multiple unexpected transactions
   within a short period.

3. Unexpected requests for passwords,
   PINs, or one-time verification codes.

4. Messages creating artificial urgency
   to transfer money.

5. Requests to send money to unfamiliar accounts.

6. Investment offers promising guaranteed
   high returns with little or no risk.

TRANSACTION ANOMALY DETECTION

Transaction anomaly detection identifies
patterns that differ from normal financial activity.

Examples include:

- Unusually large transactions
- Unexpected transaction frequency
- Transactions at unusual times
- Changes in spending patterns
- Multiple similar transactions

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

An unusual transaction is not necessarily fraudulent.

Anomaly detection identifies transactions
that may require additional investigation.

A financial analytics system should not
automatically classify every anomaly as fraud.

FALSE POSITIVES

A false positive occurs when legitimate
financial activity is incorrectly flagged
as suspicious.

For example:

A customer makes a large purchase while traveling.

The transaction may differ from their normal
spending behavior but still be legitimate.

FRAUD PREVENTION PRACTICES

1. Enable transaction notifications.
2. Use strong and unique passwords.
3. Enable multi-factor authentication.
4. Avoid sharing PINs or verification codes.
5. Review bank statements regularly.
6. Verify payment requests independently.
7. Contact the financial institution through
   an official channel when suspicious
   activity is identified.

ROLE OF AI IN FRAUD AWARENESS

AI systems can analyze transaction patterns
and identify unusual financial activity.

Machine learning models may use features such as:

- Transaction amount
- Transaction frequency
- Transaction category
- Transaction timestamp
- Historical spending patterns

AI-generated fraud alerts should be treated
as indicators requiring verification.

IMPORTANT CONSIDERATIONS

Transaction datasets may be incomplete.

Unusual activity does not prove fraudulent intent.

Financial institutions may use additional
information unavailable to analytics applications.

FinSight AI provides financial education
and anomaly awareness, not definitive
fraud determinations.
