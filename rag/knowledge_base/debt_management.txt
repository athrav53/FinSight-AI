DEBT MANAGEMENT GUIDE

WHAT IS DEBT MANAGEMENT?

Debt management is the process of tracking,
organizing, and repaying borrowed money.

Effective debt management helps individuals
understand outstanding balances, interest costs,
and repayment obligations.

COMMON TYPES OF DEBT

1. Credit Card Debt:
   Money owed through credit card purchases.

2. Personal Loans:
   Borrowed money repaid through agreed installments.

3. Student Loans:
   Loans used to finance educational expenses.

4. Home Loans:
   Loans used to purchase residential property.

5. Vehicle Loans:
   Loans used to purchase vehicles.

IMPORTANT DEBT TERMS

Principal:
The original amount borrowed.

Interest:
The cost of borrowing money.

Outstanding Balance:
The amount still owed on a debt.

Interest Rate:
The percentage charged on borrowed money.

Minimum Payment:
The minimum amount required by a lender
for a particular payment period.

DEBT-TO-INCOME RATIO

Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI) measures monthly
debt payments relative to gross monthly income.

DTI Formula:

DTI = (Total Monthly Debt Payments /
       Gross Monthly Income) * 100

EXAMPLE

Monthly Debt Payments: Rs. 12,000
Gross Monthly Income: Rs. 60,000

DTI = (12,000 / 60,000) * 100

DTI = 20%

DEBT SNOWBALL METHOD

The debt snowball method prioritizes
paying off debts with the smallest balances.

Minimum payments are maintained on other debts.

After clearing the smallest debt,
additional repayment money is directed
toward the next smallest debt.

This method may provide motivational benefits.

DEBT AVALANCHE METHOD

The debt avalanche method prioritizes
debts with the highest interest rates.

Minimum payments are maintained on other debts.

Additional repayment money is directed
toward the highest-interest debt first.

This approach generally reduces total
interest costs compared with the snowball
method when other relevant conditions are equal.

SNOWBALL VS AVALANCHE

Snowball:
Prioritizes the smallest outstanding balance.

Avalanche:
Prioritizes the highest interest rate.

The best approach depends on financial
circumstances and repayment behavior.

DEBT MANAGEMENT BEST PRACTICES

1. Track all outstanding debts.
2. Record interest rates and payment deadlines.
3. Make required minimum payments on time.
4. Avoid unnecessary high-interest borrowing.
5. Review monthly debt repayment progress.
6. Consider total borrowing costs and fees.
7. Maintain a realistic repayment budget.

IMPORTANT CONSIDERATIONS

Debt repayment strategies do not guarantee
a specific repayment timeline.

Interest rates and loan terms may change.

Missing payments may result in fees
or other financial consequences.

A low debt-to-income ratio does not
automatically indicate financial security.
