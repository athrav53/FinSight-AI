PERSONAL FINANCIAL PLANNING GUIDE

WHAT IS FINANCIAL PLANNING?

Financial planning is the process of evaluating
current financial circumstances, setting goals,
and developing strategies to manage money.

Financial planning helps individuals make
informed decisions about income, expenses,
savings, debt, and future financial needs.

KEY COMPONENTS OF FINANCIAL PLANNING

1. Income Management:
   Understanding regular and irregular income.

2. Expense Management:
   Tracking essential and discretionary spending.

3. Budgeting:
   Creating a plan for allocating income.

4. Emergency Savings:
   Preparing for unexpected necessary expenses.

5. Debt Management:
   Monitoring outstanding balances and repayments.

6. Insurance Planning:
   Evaluating financial risks and protection needs.

7. Retirement Planning:
   Preparing financially for later stages of life.

8. Investment Planning:
   Understanding investment goals, risks,
   and time horizons.

FINANCIAL GOAL SETTING

Financial goals can be classified as:

Short-Term Goals:
Goals expected to be achieved within one year.

Medium-Term Goals:
Goals expected to be achieved within one to five years.

Long-Term Goals:
Goals expected to take more than five years.

NET WORTH CALCULATION

Net Worth = Total Assets - Total Liabilities

Assets may include:
- Cash
- Bank savings
- Investments
- Property
- Other valuable possessions

Liabilities may include:
- Outstanding loans
- Credit card balances
- Mortgages
- Other financial obligations

EXAMPLE

Total Assets: Rs. 800,000
Total Liabilities: Rs. 300,000

Net Worth = 800,000 - 300,000

Net Worth = Rs. 500,000

FINANCIAL PLANNING PROCESS

Step 1:
Review income, expenses, assets, and liabilities.

Step 2:
Identify financial priorities and goals.

Step 3:
Create a realistic budget.

Step 4:
Develop savings and debt repayment strategies.

Step 5:
Consider financial risks and protection needs.

Step 6:
Monitor progress toward financial goals.

Step 7:
Review and update the financial plan regularly.

FINANCIAL HEALTH INDICATORS

Common financial health indicators include:

1. Savings rate
2. Debt-to-income ratio
3. Net worth
4. Emergency savings
5. Monthly cash flow
6. Expense trends

IMPORTANT CONSIDERATIONS

Financial circumstances differ between individuals.

A positive net worth does not necessarily mean
that someone has sufficient accessible cash.

Financial plans should be reviewed when income,
expenses, responsibilities, or goals change.

Investment performance is uncertain.

This document provides general financial
education and not personalized financial advice.
